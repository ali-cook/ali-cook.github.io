Automation · Apps Script

Building an AI testing tool when Engineering was at capacity

Setting up an assessment went from an hour or more to about a minute.

Time saved: 1+ hr → ~1 minBuild time: ~1-2 hrsAdopted by: CX, Sales, Product
read the storyclose
The problem

Testing AI-powered FAQ tools built on Josef Q meant pasting 100+ questions in one by one, waiting up to a minute for each response, then copying it back into a spreadsheet to assess. It took tens of hours a week from customer-facing teams, and Engineering had no capacity to help.

What I did

As a non-engineer, I used ChatGPT to write an Apps Script that sent each question in the spreadsheet to the tool's API endpoint and wrote the answer from the response back into it, ready for the team to assess. It was set up so anyone could point it at a different Josef Q tool just by entering its tool ID, so it was easy to reuse.

What changed

CX, Sales and Product adopted it straight away. It ran in every Josef Q pilot and made it quick to show customers their tool's reliability climbing, commonly from 60-70% to around 90% after one or two rounds of moderation.

GenAI · evaluation

Creating a framework for legal and compliance teams to judge AI risk and reliability

A simple reliability scale that became the standard way customers assessed Josef Q.

Reach: ~50+ customers & prospectsConverted POCs: adidas and others
read the storyclose
The problem

Customers and prospects didn't know how to judge whether their AI-powered FAQ tools were reliable enough to surface legal and compliance content directly to non-expert users. They struggled to manage that risk and were often bogged down trying to perfect each answer, an impossible task at scale. It blocked deals from progressing and new tools from launching.

What I did

I helped design an internal reliability framework with Cornell and NYU that scored test outputs on accuracy and clarity, giving each Josef Q tool an overall reliability score. With CX, I then turned it into a simple 3-point scale customers and prospects could use to assess the reliability of their own tools. It was specifically designed to help them separate out real risk from outputs that just needed polish.

What changed

Teams shifted from chasing perfect answers to managing real risk, gaining a clear sense of their tools' reliability and helping deals and launches progress. The scale was used in multiple POCs that converted, including adidas and Australian Retirement Trust, and became part of customer onboarding and education on working with and assessing AI.

GenAI · build vs buy

Helping a buyer decide whether to build or buy legal AI

Designed and ran the bake-off that led the American Arbitration Association to choose Josef Q over building in-house.

Verified: blind cross-check by AAAOutcome: AAA chose to buy, not build
read the storyclose
The problem

The American Arbitration Association (AAA) had started building its own in-house AI tool, and was deciding whether to invest the developer resources to keep building or buy Josef Q. A key question for them was whether Josef Q could be as reliable as an internally built tool.

What I did

I designed and ran a bake-off between Josef Q and AAA's in-house tool. Using my automated testing tool and the reliability framework I co-developed, I ran three rounds of tests on ~200-300 questions to compare both tools fairly. The rounds were designed to highlight key Josef Q features, like response moderation and its impact on reliability. I also taught AAA's team the framework so they could check my scoring blind.

What changed

The bake-off showed Josef Q was as reliable as AAA's own tool or better, and highlighted its differentiating features, which led to Josef winning the deal. In a fireside chat, AAA's Chief Innovation Officer later pointed to governance and repeated testing as the reason to buy.

Enablement · training

Teaching lawyers to build their own legal tech

Reached 1000+ legal professionals, and became a module in the College of Law (Australia) course.

read the storyclose
The problem

Onboarding meant intensive one-to-one help, and demand was growing faster than the team. From running frontline support, I could see exactly where people got stuck.

What I did

Redesigned and relaunched the Josef Designer & Builder Program: 6-week cohorts on legal design and process mapping, ending with participants building and submitting their own tool. Ran 3 public cohorts and private cohorts for enterprise legal teams with CX.

What changed

One-to-one support became education at scale. Some participants launched the tools they built. The program became a standalone offering, a lever in enterprise expansions and renewals, and the main training route for smaller customers, which freed up CX time.

Leadership · mentoring

Helping law students think like builders

They went from routing work through me to working directly with Sales and CX.

The problem

Our 3 junior Legal Technologists were all law students. Law school trains you to spot every possible issue, but building tools for real users means deciding which risks actually matter.
